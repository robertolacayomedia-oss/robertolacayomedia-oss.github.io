Father's Day — the hands that hold us.

A 60-second, zero-budget idea for national television that grew into a 1:45 cinematic short. Here is the whole path: brief, concept, the options I didn't choose, moodboard, script, shot list and storyboard — next to the frames we actually shot.

01

Brief

What the channel needed.

An emotional Father's Day spot for VTV's national broadcast — made in-house, without hiring actors or renting locations.

The constraint shaped the idea: everything had to be shot inside the channel's own facilities, with the people who already work there.

ClientVTV Honduras
Format60″ promo → 1:45 cut + 0:33 cut

My roleConcept, script, direction, edit
02

Concept

One visual idea, four options on the table.

"Las manos que nos sostienen" — tell the story only through a father's hands. No faces until the end, so every viewer projects their own dad onto the screen.

Hands are intimate, universal and cheap to shoot: close-ups hide the background, so we didn't need sets. I pitched it alongside three alternatives so the channel could compare tone, cost and complexity.

Chosen

The hands that hold us

Detail shots of everyday care and work — tying shoes, fixing a toy, driving — until the first face appears in a hug.

Alternative 1

The adult son

Role reversal: tired hands at work, then a younger hand brings coffee and rests on his shoulder.

Alternative 2

POV camera

Wide lens at a child's eye level: the father dresses, guides and cheers "the viewer" directly.

Alternative 3

Presence through absence

Only objects: a packed lunchbox, a fixed bike, a child's drawing straightened on the fridge.

Framing

80% detail shots and close-ups. Intimacy, and no set dressing needed.

Light & lens

Natural window light bounced in, a backlight to carve the texture of the hands. 50 mm or longer for a soft, cinematic background.

Music

Acoustic indie folk. Soft guitar at the start; strings or piano lift in at the hug for the emotional peak.

07

From paper to screen

What changed in production and in the edit.

The broken toy became an origami parrot — a gift from the son that travels with his father all day and turns the montage into a story.

  • A broken toy being fixed→The origami parrot as a recurring object
  • Five separate "hands" vignettes→One day in a father's life, morning to playground
  • One 60″ spot→A 1:45 cinematic cut and a 0:33 promo cut
