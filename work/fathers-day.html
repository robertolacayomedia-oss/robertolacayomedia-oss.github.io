Father's Day at VTV

During my internship at VTV Honduras the channel asked me for a Father's Day spot. No actors, no budget, and we had to film it in the building. This page shows how I planned it, and what changed once we started filming.

01

The brief

VTV wanted something that would make people feel something on Father's Day, on national TV.

There was no money for actors or locations. So everything had to happen inside the channel, with people who already worked there and their kids.

ClientVTV Honduras
Length60 sec planned, 1:45 in the end
BudgetNone
What I didIdea, script, directing, edit
02

The idea

Only show a dad's hands. Tying shoes, fixing a toy, driving, working. You don't see a face until the very end, so everyone watching can picture their own dad.

Close-ups of hands also saved us. You don't see the room behind them, so we didn't need a set. In Spanish I called it "Las manos que nos sostienen", the hands that hold us.

I brought three other ideas to the meeting too, in case the channel wanted something else:

The one we made

The hands

Small moments of a dad caring and working, until the first face shows up in a hug.

Idea 2

The grown-up son

An older dad, tired after work. A young hand puts a coffee next to him and rests on his shoulder.

Idea 3

Through a kid's eyes

The camera is the child. Dad bends down, fixes your collar, points the way, says you can do it.

Idea 4

Only objects

A packed lunchbox, a bike put back up, a kid's drawing straightened on the fridge. Dad is never really there.

Shots

Mostly close-ups. Hands, faces, small objects.

Light

Daylight from the windows, plus one light from behind so you can see the skin on the hands. A 50mm or longer to blur the background.

Music

Soft acoustic guitar. When the kid runs into his dad's arms, strings or piano come in.

07

What changed

From the plan to the film.

The broken toy turned into a paper parrot. The son gives it to his dad at the start of the day, and the dad carries it around with him: in the car, on his desk, and back to pick his son up.

That one object tied the small moments together into a single day. The spot grew from 60 seconds to 1:45, and we also cut a shorter 33 second version.
